# How to free up space on your Mac when storage is full

Open `System Settings > General > Storage` to see what fills your Mac, then work from the biggest category down. Empty the Trash, clear old downloads and large files you no longer need, remove apps you don't use along with their leftover files, and clear app caches. Those four steps cover most of what a full disk is made of, and none of them need special software.

## Step 1: see what is using the space

1. Click the Apple menu, then `System Settings > General > Storage`.
2. Wait for the colored bar to finish calculating.
3. Click the info button next to a category, such as `Documents` or `Applications`, to see its biggest items.

Note the two or three largest categories. Spend your time there first, because deleting many small files rarely changes much.

## Step 2: empty the Trash and clear Downloads

Files in the Trash still use disk space until you empty it.

1. In Finder, choose `Finder > Empty Trash…`. Look through the Trash first, since this cannot be undone.
2. Open `~/Downloads` and sort by size (`View > as List`, then click the `Size` column). Installer files (`.dmg`, `.pkg`) and old zip archives are usually safe to remove once the app is installed.
3. In `System Settings > General > Storage`, turn on `Empty Trash Automatically` if you want items older than 30 days removed for you.

## Step 3: find large files you no longer need

In `System Settings > General > Storage`, click the info button next to `Documents`. The `Large Files` list shows the biggest items on the Mac. Select anything you recognize and no longer need, then choose `Delete`.

For a manual search, open Finder, choose `File > Find`, set the first filter to `File Size`, and pick `is greater than`. Large video exports, disk images and old project folders tend to show up here.

Some of these views delete items permanently instead of moving them to the Trash, so check the name before you confirm.

## Step 4: remove apps you don't use

1. Open `System Settings > General > Storage` and click the info button next to `Applications` to see apps by size.
2. Drag unwanted apps from `/Applications` to the Trash, then empty it.
3. Apps leave support files behind. Check these folders for items named after the app or its maker:
   - `~/Library/Application Support`
   - `~/Library/Caches`
   - `~/Library/Preferences`
   - `~/Library/Containers`

Only delete a folder when you are sure it belongs to an app you have removed. Leftovers from an app you still use can reset its settings or remove its data.

## Step 5: clear caches and developer build files

Caches are copies that apps and tools rebuild when they need them, so they are safe to remove with the app closed.

- `~/Library/Caches`: quit the app first, then delete the contents of that app's subfolder. Leave the `Caches` folder itself in place.
- `~/Library/Developer/Xcode/DerivedData`: Xcode build output. Quit Xcode and delete the contents. The next build recreates what it needs.
- `node_modules` inside old project folders: delete it, and `npm install` restores it when you return to the project.
- Docker: `docker system prune` removes stopped containers, unused networks and dangling images. Read the confirmation prompt before you accept it.

## Step 6: check backups, Mail and Photos

- Old device backups: `System Settings > General > Storage`, then look for `iOS Files` and remove backups of devices you no longer own.
- Mail: in the Mail app, attachments take space. Choose `Mail > Settings… > Accounts` and review the download setting for each account.
- Photos: items in the `Recently Deleted` album stay for 30 days and still count toward storage. Open the album and choose `Delete All` if you are sure.
- Time Machine local snapshots: `tmutil listlocalsnapshots /` lists them. macOS removes these on its own when space is needed, so you rarely have to act.

## What not to delete

- Anything in `/System` or `/private/var`.
- The contents of a Photos or Music library package. Manage those inside the app.
- Mail data under `~/Library/Mail`.
- Folders in `~/Library/Application Support` or `~/Library/Containers` that belong to apps you still use.
- Files you cannot identify. Leave them, or search the name first.

## FAQ

### Why is System Data so large?

System Data holds caches, logs, local snapshots and other files that macOS and apps create. It grows over time. Clearing app caches under `~/Library/Caches` reduces part of it, but do not delete system folders by hand.

### Is it safe to delete everything in `~/Library/Caches`?

Usually, with the apps closed, because apps recreate their caches. Delete the contents of individual app folders and keep the `Caches` folder itself. If an app behaves oddly afterward, restart it and it will rebuild what it needs.
